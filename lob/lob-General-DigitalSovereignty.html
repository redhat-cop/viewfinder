Data Sovereignty

Organizations should classify and appropriately localize sensitive data according to relevant privacy regulations and business requirements. Implement strong encryption and access controls, ensuring your organization maintains control over cryptographic keys rather than delegating this to external service providers. Establish clear data residency policies and ensure contracts include provisions for data repatriation if needed.

Technical Sovereignty

Reduce vendor lock-in by adopting open standards and vendor-agnostic technologies where possible. Consider multi-cloud or hybrid strategies to ensure workload portability and maintain flexibility. Build internal expertise in core technologies like identity management, networking, and security to maintain operational independence and reduce reliance on any single vendor.

Operational Sovereignty

Maintain direct control over critical operational functions including system configuration, security monitoring, and incident response. Develop in-house capabilities for essential operations and ensure you can independently operate and recover systems without external dependencies. Establish tested exit strategies that demonstrate your ability to migrate from third-party services if needed.

Managed Services

When leveraging managed services, ensure your organization retains ownership and control over critical elements such as identity management, encryption keys, and administrative access. Use contracts that clearly define data processing locations, specify the governing legal jurisdiction, and guarantee your ability to audit and retrieve data. Avoid arrangements where service providers have unilateral control over your critical infrastructure.

Assurance Sovereignty

Establish contractual rights to conduct audits and independently validate the security, compliance, and operational controls of external service providers. Use transparent monitoring tools that provide continuous visibility into system integrity and compliance status. Ensure you have access to logs, metrics, and audit trails necessary to demonstrate compliance with applicable regulations.

Executive Oversight

Treat Digital Sovereignty as a strategic priority with executive-level oversight and accountability. Integrate sovereignty considerations into enterprise risk management frameworks and ensure adequate budget and resources are allocated. Establish clear governance structures that include legal, IT, and business stakeholders to make informed decisions about technology dependencies and vendor relationships.

Open Source

Actively leverage Open Source Software (OSS) to benefit from transparency, auditability, and community innovation. Establish a robust OSS governance framework that manages security, licensing, and maintenance of open-source components. Contributing to OSS projects relevant to your operations can help ensure they remain secure, well-maintained, and aligned with your needs.
